Empirical distributions

The MIT corpus also has heavy upper tails, but the full graph is not established as scale free

Both complementary cumulative distributions use logarithmic axes. The fitted line begins at the data-selected cutoff. Statistical inference follows the same procedure as the HEART analysis.

Interpretation. Neither fitted tail is rejected at the predeclared 0.10 level. The lognormal sign is favoured for both measures, and the weighted-degree alternative is distinguishable at 0.10 but not at 0.05. This supports heavy-tail recurrence under dataset substitution, but not a universal power-law mechanism.

Calculation and mathematical specification

The HEART procedure is rerun without tuning to MIT

Let zi denote the BGE-M3 vector for MIT record i. The same graph and tail tests are applied without changing thresholds after viewing the MIT result.

  1. Source extraction.The official v4 workbook was retrieved on 21 October 2026. The analysis includes rows labelled Risk Category or Risk Sub-Category and excludes Paper and Additional evidence rows. Source row numbers, Ev_ID values, framework references, domains, and subdomains are retained.
  2. Semantic graph.Cosine similarity is sij = (zi · zj)/(‖zi‖2‖zj‖2). The undirected graph is the union of each record's eight nearest neighbours where sij ≥ 0.60.
  3. Connectivity.Degree is ki = Σj 1[(i,j) ∈ E]. Strength is wi = Σj:(i,j)∈E sij. Domain-crossing status compares the MIT Domain field at both endpoints.
  4. Tail fitting.Candidate lower cutoffs retain at least 50 observations. Maximum-likelihood exponents are estimated, and the cutoff minimizing the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance is selected.
  5. Validation.One thousand semi-parametric bootstrap samples test goodness-of-fit. A lower-truncated lognormal fit and two-sided Vuong comparison test whether the power law is distinguishable from a plausible heavy-tailed alternative.

Reproducibility and validation

Source, transformation, graph, and output checks

Official source preservation

The unmodified MIT XLSX is stored with its retrieval date, sheet name, CC BY 4.0 licence, and SHA-256 fingerprint. The processed CSV retains every included source row and provenance field.

Extraction audit

The test independently recounts 570 risk-category and 1,265 risk-subcategory rows, for 1,835 analysis records. Source-local Ev_ID collisions are preserved and disambiguated with stable analysis IDs.

Graph reconstruction

The test regenerates the nearest-neighbour union from the embedding matrix, verifies every threshold-qualified edge and weight, and checks that Σk = 2|E|.

Controlled settings

The BGE-M3 revision, 1,024-dimensional vectors, eight-neighbour rule, 0.60 cutoff, seed, 1,000 bootstraps, and 50-record minimum tail are identical to HEART.

Meaning and limits

What the ablation adds

Academic significance

Heavy upper tails and cross-domain semantic bridges recur after replacing HEART with an independently assembled multi-framework repository. This weakens the explanation that the observed concentration is only an artefact of HEART's three-domain design. It does not identify a universal generative law.

Practical significance

Highly connected records can prompt reviewers to inspect adjacent risk mechanisms beyond an initial domain label. For risk discovery, the network is best used as a boundary-expansion device alongside the curated hierarchy, not as an automatic severity or priority score.

Limitations

MIT records differ in granularity, repeat concepts across source frameworks, and use seven thematic domains rather than HEART's General, Agentic, and Physical operating domains. Cross-domain percentages therefore demonstrate boundary crossing within each ontology but should not be treated as directly interchangeable effect sizes.
