AI risk incidents

In preparation. This page will collect newly reported AI incidents on an ongoing basis and analyse them using HEART. Real-time collection and incident results are not yet available.

Concept and scope

An AI risk describes a potential harm; an AI incident is a reported real-world event in which an AI system caused or contributed to harm. Near misses, where harm was narrowly avoided, will be recorded separately. This distinction follows the AI Incident Database's focus on real-world harms and near harms.

Coverage will span General, Agentic and Physical AI, including system failures, misuse and harmful interactions affecting people, organisations, society or the environment. Hypothetical scenarios, model benchmarks and unsupported claims will not be treated as confirmed incidents. Reported allegations and uncertain AI involvement will remain explicitly labelled.

Planned analysis

Source reports will be checked and deduplicated, then mapped to relevant HEART L1 domains, L2 risk areas, L3 categories and L4 cards. Each record will retain source links, event and reporting dates, affected parties, reported harms and the basis for its taxonomy mapping. Analysis will support learning from failures and identifying mitigation needs, not estimates of incident prevalence or proof of causality.

Primary sources

The planned evidence base is the AI Incident Database (AIID), maintained by the Responsible AI Collaborative, and its OECD.AI catalogue entry. The OECD link describes the same AIID, not a separate incident database or OECD endorsement. Original reports will remain linked for verification. Coverage will reflect available reporting and may be incomplete or delayed.
