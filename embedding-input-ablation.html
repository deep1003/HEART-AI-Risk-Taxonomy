Controlled assignment ablation

Accuracy depends on the embedding model, taxonomy, language, and text fields

This experiment evaluates two multilingual embedding models on two independently structured AI-risk taxonomies under four fixed input conditions: English title, English title plus definition, Korean title, and Korean title plus definition. All comparisons use the same cosine-retrieval rule and are reported against the taxonomy's existing category assignment.

Paired effect

Adding definitions produces the largest and most stable improvement

Each interval is a 95% paired bootstrap interval for the change in Top-1 agreement when a definition is added to the title. Exact McNemar tests use the same item-level predictions and correct for pairing.

Interpretation. The definition gain is positive for both models, both taxonomies, and both languages. Model choice changes some individual assignments, but the effect of adding definitional context is larger and more consistent than the BGE-M3 versus E5 difference.

Language condition

Korean inputs yield lower agreement in most cells

For HEART, Korean fields are the official bilingual taxonomy text. For MIT, the public English source was translated with a fixed NLLB model, so the MIT Korean result is a translation-mediated condition rather than a comparison of two official source languages.

HEART

Switching from English to Korean changes Top-1 agreement by −2.25 to −5.31 percentage points, depending on model and text field. Three of four differences are statistically distinguishable at 0.05; the BGE-M3 title-only difference is not.

The translation-mediated Korean conditions are 5.43 to 9.13 percentage points below English. All four paired differences are statistically distinguishable. This combines language sensitivity with translation effects and must not be interpreted as a general ranking of languages.

Model comparison

BGE-M3 and E5 differ by −5.47 to +2.05 percentage points across cells. Most model contrasts are not statistically distinguishable; the HEART Korean title-only cell favours BGE-M3.

Design and mathematical specification

A frozen nearest-category retrieval experiment

The experiment is deliberately separate from the earlier scale-law analysis. It does not construct or refit a semantic network and does not rerun EM. It tests how representation choices affect recovery of an existing hierarchical assignment.

  1. Frozen targets.HEART uses 622 validated L4 cards and 55 L3 labels. MIT uses 1,511 records with a valid leaf subdomain label and 24 leaf labels; 324 blank, domain-level, uncoded, or X-labelled records are excluded before modelling.
  2. Four input conditions.For both risk items and candidate category labels, the text is title only or title plus definition, in English or Korean. This symmetric construction prevents a richer label representation from being compared with a title-only item.
  3. Two frozen encoders.BGE-M3 produces 1,024-dimensional vectors. multilingual-E5-base produces 768-dimensional vectors and uses its documented query prefix. All vectors are L2 normalised.
  4. Assignment.For item vector zi and category vector cj, sij = zi · cj after normalisation. The predicted category is arg maxj sij. No threshold, seed label, or category prototype is tuned against the evaluation labels.
  5. Inference.Top-1 agreement is primary. Top-3, mean reciprocal rank, and macro-F1 are secondary. Item-level paired bootstrap intervals use 10,000 fixed-seed resamples; discordant Top-1 outcomes are compared with an exact McNemar test.

Integrity checks

Sequential execution and reproducible inputs

Strict stage separation

HEART × BGE-M3, MIT × BGE-M3, HEART × E5, and MIT × E5 were executed and audited as separate stages. Comparative statistics were generated only after all raw cells passed count and hash checks.

Source integrity

Every run verifies the expected item count, category count, unique IDs, prediction count, and SHA-256 of the frozen bilingual input.

Translation-length repair

An audit identified 21 MIT definitions requiring length-safe retranslation. They were reprocessed by sentence-boundary chunks of at most 350 source tokens, and all eight MIT cells were rerun against the repaired input.

Complete outputs

Machine-readable metrics, predictions, embeddings, paired tests, source manifests, translation metadata, and pre-repair audit outputs are retained with the experiment record.

Validated Sixteen final cells, 24 paired comparisons, and the repaired MIT input fingerprint were verified before website integration.

Meaning and limitations

What the ablation supports

Academic significance

The effect of definitional context generalises across two encoders and two taxonomies. Representation choices are therefore part of the measurement model, not a neutral preprocessing detail. Cross-taxonomy replication strengthens this conclusion without implying equivalence between the ontologies.

Practical significance

Automated candidate mapping should embed complete risk definitions whenever available and retain language-specific validation. Title-only retrieval may be useful for rapid screening, but it should not be treated as a substitute for definition-aware human review.

Limitations

The reference labels are curated assignments rather than an external ground truth. MIT Korean results include machine-translation error. The experiment tests direct nearest-category retrieval, not EM, fine-tuning, reranking, or expert adjudication. Taxonomy sizes and granularity differ, so absolute accuracy should be compared cautiously.
