About HEART

Technical Report (reviewed edition, 39 pages) · LaTeX source

Why HEART

HEART provides a shared, evidence-linked vocabulary for risks spanning AI model outputs, delegated decisions and physical actions, supporting service-specific assessment without redefining categories.

Data and structure

HEART contains 622 expert-rewritten bilingual risk cards across 47 independently designed, literature-based categories in General AI (492), Agentic AI (67) and Physical AI (63). Its five-level hierarchy connects Responsible AI, operating domains, risk areas, categories and evidence-linked cards. The cards and their expert-confirmed assignments are a fixed reference, not a uniquely correct taxonomy or an algorithmic target.

Methods

Algorithm 1. Top-down categories are combined with evidence-based candidates. The experiment evaluates repeated semantic grouping, retaining a representative from each group. Experts rewrote, merged and removed the F5 output to produce the 622 cards; automated grouping is not expert adjudication.

Algorithm 2. Cards are assigned to eligible L3 categories by embedding similarity, then definition-anchored centroids are updated iteratively. The experiment compares this numerical procedure with expert assignments; it does not automate every expert boundary rule. Website links, layout and filters are separate exploration methods and do not reclassify cards.

Key results

With the same complete bilingual names and definitions and known L1 domains, Algorithm 2 iterative Top-1 reference agreement is 69.45% for BGE-M3 (432/622) and 62.70% for multilingual-E5-base (390/622). Fresh Algorithm 1 F5 inventories contain 817 and 875 representatives. Historical F5 shows semantic proximity to the rewritten reference, but neither this proximity nor a count near 622 establishes semantic correctness or an optimal stopping stage.

The report distinguishes computational results from semantic validity. Some original encoding and diagnostic details remain incompletely recoverable. The reviewed edition changes no evaluation score or risk card.

Evaluation scope, sensitivity and limitations

The construction baseline reports 82,971 risk-focused source records (50,375 publications, 938 policy/technical reports and 31,658 patents), not incidents. The new experiments do not reproduce upstream collection, screening or initial candidate generation. Both encoders receive matched text without IDs or hierarchy labels; primary inputs fit a 512-token limit. Tokenisation, pooling and model training still differ.

Construction versus exploration. The TR describes upstream HDBSCAN grouping and class-based TF-IDF summaries, followed by constrained consolidation and EM-style assignment. Expert adjudication determines the released cards. Separately, the website links the undirected union of eight nearest neighbours with BGE-M3 cosine similarity ≥ 0.60 and uses ForceAtlas2 layout. Color reflects L1/L3 semantic shades; size reflects weighted degree, not risk priority.

Assignment sensitivity. Across 192 conditions, input language, definitions, truncation, update rules and perturbations affect agreement. Bilingual names alone yield 42.12%/41.16%; a 128-token limit yields 63.99%/61.41% (BGE/E5). Whole-inventory refinement uses known L1 and has no held-out unseen-card test. Some cap/tolerance terminations are not fixed points.

Consolidation and F5. The construction trajectory reaches 792 F5 representatives before expert rewriting. Matched-input re-encoding yields 817 with BGE-M3 and 875 with E5; cross-model partition ARI is 0.2527, showing that similar counts need not mean similar groupings. The two evaluators select the same nearest F5 representative for 517/622 reference cards (83.12%). This is retrieval-view agreement, not semantic accuracy or recall; many-to-many rewrites require independent review.

Reproducibility. Replaying retained embedding vectors reproduces grouping counts and thresholds, not the original encoding process. Original model execution and parts of the diagnostic calculations remain incompletely verified. This limits historical reconstruction, without establishing or refuting semantic correctness.

MIT appendix. The saved MIT version-4 extract contains 1,810 risk statements from 74 paper IDs. Forty-four source titles overlap with HEART, so replication demonstrates related-corpus structural repeatability, not fully independent semantic validation or a fresh two-model comparison.

Reference verification. Access to some complete original texts and paid ISO normative clauses remains limited. Bibliographic or official-scope checks do not establish every empirical claim or standards conformity. The earlier citation-review record remains available as historical evidence; the current edition removes five references and their dependent claims. See the current release metadata.

Keywords & applications mapping

Keywords combine semantic similarity with scope checks; domain filters follow L1 assignments, and applications use capability-based rules. Filters identify candidate risks without changing the taxonomy.

Technical process and AI for Everyone (Korea) example

Keywords. Source-grounded concept names and definitions are embedded with the same pinned BGE-M3 model as the English L4 names and definitions. The 1,024-dimensional vectors are L2-normalised; their dot product gives cosine similarity against all 622 cards. A card is selected when its score meets the concept-specific threshold (currently 0.60–0.75) or it is explicitly included by a documented scope review, unless explicitly excluded. General AI, Agentic AI and Physical AI instead match existing L1 assignments exactly. These retrieval thresholds are separate from the graph's 0.60 link threshold and are not validated accuracy estimates.

Applications. Service capabilities are translated into deterministic applicability rules, not an application-to-card embedding score. Eligible domains are checked first; a card then matches an approved ID, category-ID prefix or term in its English name or definition, with explicit exclusions applied last. The build stores the resulting card IDs; selecting a filter highlights that set without changing taxonomy assignments or running a new classification.

AI for Everyone (Korea). The profile uses announced service plans reported on 4 September 2026 to identify chatbot responses, information search and delegated applications, bookings and payments. Rules admit General and Agentic AI cards, combining prefixes for reliability, privacy, security, inclusion and delegated authority with terms such as “accessibility”, “public service”, “payment” and “booking”. Nine specified cards are excluded; Physical AI is outside the eligible domains. The current result is 295 candidate cards (259 General, 36 Agentic). Agentic risks apply only where delegation is enabled. This is HEART's conditional applicability judgement, not a classification supplied by the news source, evidence of programme failures or a completed assessment of any deployed service.

Reproducible definitions, thresholds and scope corrections are in keyword definitions; scores and selected IDs are in keyword mappings. Application rules are in SCENARIOS and match(), with published memberships in the network data.

How to use it

Filter by keyword or application, then select a node for its definition, hierarchy and evidence. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and double-click the background to reset. The right panel shows an overview until a card is selected.

Use AI risk taxonomy to browse the hierarchy. For assessment, confirm applicable risks, controls and accountable owners against the service's actual capabilities. AI risk incidents describes planned monitoring; collection is not yet active.

Limits

Published evidence and expert judgement limit coverage. Reference agreement is not independent objective accuracy; F5 proximity is not verified semantic equivalence. Original execution history, some original full texts and ISO normative clauses remain incompletely verified. Encoder, input and numerical choices affect results, and superiority over alternative construction methods is not established. Network proximity is not causality; size and color do not indicate severity or likelihood. Probability and Severity remain unassigned. Application profiles require service-specific validation.
