About HEART

Why HEART

HEART provides a shared, evidence-linked vocabulary for risks spanning AI model outputs, delegated decisions and physical actions, supporting service-specific assessment without redefining categories.

Data and structure

HEART contains 622 bilingual risk cards across 47 categories in General AI (492), Agentic AI (67) and Physical AI (63). Its five-level hierarchy connects Responsible AI ownership, domains, risk areas, categories and evidence-linked cards.

Methods

Top-down frameworks and standards are combined with bottom-up evidence. Semantic grouping and iterative mapping support expert adjudication, which determines final classifications. The network links similar card embeddings; color reflects domain and category shades, while size reflects weighted degree.

Keywords & applications mapping

Keywords combine semantic similarity with scope checks; domain filters follow L1 assignments, and applications use capability-based rules. Filters identify candidate risks without changing the taxonomy.

Technical process and AI for Everyone (Korea) example

Keywords. Source-grounded concept names and definitions are embedded with the same pinned BGE-M3 model as the English L4 names and definitions. The 1,024-dimensional vectors are L2-normalised; their dot product gives cosine similarity against all 622 cards. A card is selected when its score meets the concept-specific threshold (currently 0.60–0.75) or it is explicitly included by a documented scope review, unless explicitly excluded. General AI, Agentic AI and Physical AI instead match existing L1 assignments exactly. These retrieval thresholds are separate from the graph's 0.60 link threshold and are not validated accuracy estimates.

Applications. Service capabilities are translated into deterministic applicability rules, not an application-to-card embedding score. Eligible domains are checked first; a card then matches an approved ID, category-ID prefix or term in its English name or definition, with explicit exclusions applied last. The build stores the resulting card IDs; selecting a filter highlights that set without changing taxonomy assignments or running a new classification.

AI for Everyone (Korea). The profile uses announced service plans reported on 4 September 2026 to identify chatbot responses, information search and delegated applications, bookings and payments. Rules admit General and Agentic AI cards, combining prefixes for reliability, privacy, security, inclusion and delegated authority with terms such as “accessibility”, “public service”, “payment” and “booking”. Nine specified cards are excluded; Physical AI is outside the eligible domains. The current result is 295 candidate cards (259 General, 36 Agentic). Agentic risks apply only where delegation is enabled. This is HEART's conditional applicability judgement, not a classification supplied by the news source, evidence of programme failures or a completed assessment of any deployed service.

Reproducible definitions, thresholds and scope corrections are in keyword definitions; scores and selected IDs are in keyword mappings. Application rules are in SCENARIOS and match(), with published memberships in the network data.

How to use it

Filter by keyword or application, then select a node for its definition, hierarchy and evidence. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and double-click the background to reset. The right panel shows an overview until a card is selected.

Use AI risk taxonomy to browse the hierarchy. For assessment, confirm applicable risks, controls and accountable owners against the service's actual capabilities. AI risk incidents describes planned monitoring; collection is not yet active.

Limits

Coverage depends on available evidence; independent classification accuracy and reviewer agreement are not established. Network proximity is not causality, and size or color does not indicate severity or likelihood. Application profiles require service-specific validation.
