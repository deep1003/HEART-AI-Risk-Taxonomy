About HEART

HEART provides 622 bilingual, evidence-linked risk cards in 47 categories across General, Agentic and Physical AI. It offers a shared vocabulary for identifying risks in model outputs, delegated decisions and physical actions. Each card connects a risk definition with its hierarchy and supporting evidence. Experts independently designed the literature-based categories and rewrote the cards; their assignments serve as a reference, not a uniquely correct taxonomy.

Key results

Category assignment. With complete bilingual names and definitions and known L1 domains, Algorithm 2 achieves Top-1 expert-reference agreement of 69.45% for BGE-M3 (432/622) and 62.70% for multilingual-E5-base (390/622). These measure agreement, not independent accuracy.

Iterative consolidation. Algorithm 1's fifth grouping iteration (F5) produced 792 representatives, which experts subsequently rewrote into 622 cards. Semantic correspondence supports comparison with that rewrite, but does not establish equivalence or an optimal stopping point. Results depend on the embedding model and input text.

Method and interpretation

The construction evidence comprises 82,971 publications, reports and patent records, not incidents. HDBSCAN grouping and class-based TF-IDF summaries inform candidate generation; Algorithm 1 repeatedly groups similar candidates. Algorithm 2 assigns cards by embedding similarity within eligible domains and iteratively refines definition-anchored category centroids. Its evaluation implements numerical constraints, not every expert semantic boundary rule.

The website is a separate exploration layer. Links join the undirected union of eight nearest neighbours with BGE-M3 cosine similarity ≥ 0.60; ForceAtlas2 positions the nodes. Colour reflects hierarchy-related semantic shades and size reflects weighted degree. Neither indicates severity, likelihood or causality.

How to use it

Browse the hierarchy in AI risk taxonomy, or filter the AI risk space by keyword or application. Select a node to read its definition, hierarchy and evidence in the right panel. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan and double-click the background to reset the viewport without clearing filters. Confirm applicable risks, controls and accountable owners against actual service capabilities. AI risk incidents describes planned monitoring, not active collection.

Keywords & applications

Keywords combine semantic similarity with scope review; domains follow L1 assignments, and applications use capability-based rules. Filters highlight candidate risks without changing taxonomy assignments.

Mapping and AI for Everyone (Korea)

Keyword concepts and English card names and definitions are compared using BGE-M3 cosine similarity, concept-specific thresholds (0.60–0.75) and reviewed inclusions or exclusions. Applications instead use eligible domains, approved card or category IDs and English text terms, followed by exclusions. These are applicability rules, not validated classification scores.

AI for Everyone (Korea) uses reported service plans for chatbot responses, search and delegated applications, bookings and payments. The profile highlights 295 candidates (259 General, 36 Agentic), excluding Physical AI. Agentic risks apply only when delegation is enabled. This is HEART's conditional judgement, not evidence of programme failures or a completed service assessment.

Limits

Coverage depends on published evidence and expert judgement. Evaluation uses the fixed inventory with known domains, not a held-out test of unseen cards. Model and input sensitivity prevent claims of general superiority. Original encoding history and some full-source claims remain incompletely verified. Probability and Severity are unassigned; application profiles require service-specific validation. HEART supports assessment and review, not automatic risk scoring or certification.
