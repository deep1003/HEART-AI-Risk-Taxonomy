About HEART

Why HEART

AI services combine model outputs, delegated decisions and physical actions, exposing risks across domains. HEART provides a shared, evidence-linked vocabulary for selecting relevant risks, identifying assessment gaps and assigning controls without redefining categories for each service.

Data and structure

The Technical Report's construction baseline comprises 82,971 risk-focused source records: 50,375 scholarly publications, 938 policy and technical reports, and 31,658 patents. These are evidence records, not incidents or risk cards. The published taxonomy contains 622 bilingual cards across 47 categories: General AI (492), Agentic AI (67) and Physical AI (63).

The hierarchy is L0 Responsible AI, L1 operating domain, L2 risk area (System, Interaction or Societal Impact), L3 category and L4 risk card. Each card retains a stable ID, definition, primary hierarchy path and cited evidence.

Methods

Taxonomy construction. Prior research, governance frameworks and safety standards guide top-down category design; source evidence supplies bottom-up risk candidates. The report describes BGE-M3 embeddings, HDBSCAN density-based grouping and class-based TF-IDF keyword summaries, followed by constrained merging, splitting and deduplication. EM-style assignment iteratively matches cards to eligible L3 categories and updates definition-anchored centroids. Expert adjudication determines final classifications, not similarity alone.

Website exploration. English card names and definitions use 1,024-dimensional BGE-M3 embeddings. Links join the undirected union of eight nearest neighbours with cosine similarity ≥ 0.60; ForceAtlas2 positions the graph. Blue, orange and teal anchor the three L1 domains, with semantic shades within L3 categories. Size reflects weighted degree, the sum of link weights. Louvain communities are diagnostic only and do not define colours or taxonomy assignments.

How to use it

Select a keyword or application to highlight candidate risks and view its definition and sources. Domain filters use approved L1 assignments; other keywords use definition-to-card similarity with documented scope checks, while applications use capability-based relevance rules. Inactive risks remain pale.

Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, and double-click the background to reset the viewport without clearing filters. Zooming reveals more L4 labels. Hover for a summary; select a node to open its full card on the right, or below the graph on mobile. Zoom and pan are preserved. Use Risk cards to search or browse the hierarchy.

For assessment, define the service boundary, actors and capabilities; confirm applicable cards across inherited domains; then record evidence, tests, controls and accountable owners. Reassess when the service changes. Filters support selection, not a completed risk assessment.

Limits

The taxonomy reflects available published evidence and expert judgement; confidential or deployment-specific failures may be missing. Independent classification accuracy, reviewer agreement and superiority over alternative construction methods are not established. Graph proximity and filters indicate exploratory relevance, not causality or exhaustive coverage. Node size and colour do not measure severity, likelihood or priority. Probability and Severity remain unassigned; application profiles require service-specific validation.
