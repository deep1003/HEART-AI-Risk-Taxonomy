One taxonomy, two exploration views

The hierarchy runs from L0 Responsible AI through L1 operating domains, L2 risk areas and L3 risk categories to specific L4 risk cards. General AI, Agentic AI and Physical AI contain 492, 67 and 63 cards respectively. The three conceptual L2 risk areas are System, Interaction and Societal Impact. Their domain-specific identifiers form seven populated branches.

AI risk space reveals semantic relationships between existing cards. Risk cards combines the taxonomy tree with searchable card listings, bilingual definitions, available attributes and evidence references. These are complementary views of the same dataset, not different classifications.

A compact exploration workspace

The brand header introduces AI Risk Space across General, Agentic, and Physical AI, with HEART's full name beneath it. It scrolls out of view; only the AI risk space and Risk cards navigation row stays pinned at the top. Keywords and applications appear directly below this row, without a repeated page title. Definitions use the full available width, with supporting reference links following inline and wrapping naturally when necessary.

The graph height adapts to the browser viewport, allowing the complete network frame and the selected card to fit beneath the pinned navigation on desktop screens. Selecting a node brings this workspace into view. Zooming can deliberately move parts of the network outside the frame; reset the viewport to return to the overview. On narrower screens, the card appears below the graph instead of beside it.

How to use AI risk space

Select a keyword or application

Choose a button above the graph to highlight potentially relevant risks. The active count updates inside the graph. Unselected risks remain as pale context. Selecting another button replaces the current lens; selecting it again clears it. Reset filters clears the selection and restores the initial view.

Selections show one concise concept or service definition and at least two supporting reference links. These descriptions are indirect syntheses, not verbatim quotations or universally agreed definitions. General AI, Agentic AI and Physical AI use the existing human-approved L1 assignments; other lenses are exploratory relevance mappings. Additional references do not change existing filter memberships.

Zoom and pan

Scroll or use a trackpad pinch over the graph to zoom around the pointer. Drag the background to pan. Double-click the background to reset the viewport without clearing the selected filter. As you zoom in, more individual L4 labels appear where space permits. Label placement avoids overlaps.

For keyboard navigation, focus the graph and use + or − to zoom and Home to reset the viewport.

Inspect a risk card

Hover over an active node to see its risk card ID, English name and definition, L1–L3 hierarchy and weighted degree. Click a node or its label to open the complete card in a right-hand panel on desktop screens. The graph uses the remaining width rather than being covered by a pop-up. The panel stays beneath the pinned navigation and long card content scrolls inside it.

Select another node to replace the card and return the panel's content to the top. Close selected card restores the graph's full width without clearing its zoom, pan or selected-node highlight. Opening and switching cards also preserve the graph's zoom and pan. The expandable active-risk list provides an alternative route to the same cards. Label buttons support Enter and Space.

Explore the hierarchy

Open the Risk cards tab to browse the integrated taxonomy tree. Select an L1 domain, L2 area or L3 category to filter the cards. Search by ID, name, definition or evidence. On smaller screens, use Open taxonomy navigator to reveal the tree. The tree is not duplicated in AI risk space.

Reading the graph

A compact information row inside the graph frame shows the active-risk count, colour and size encodings, and zoom, pan and reset controls. The explanation of pale inactive nodes appears below the graph.

Position and links express semantic proximity, not observed causal propagation. English L4 names and definitions are encoded as L2-normalised, 1,024-dimensional BGE-M3 dense embeddings using Ollama 0.32.1. Links form the undirected union of each node's eight nearest neighbours, retaining cosine similarities of at least 0.60. Link weight equals cosine similarity. ForceAtlas2 provides the layout; the two-dimensional projection necessarily compresses the original semantic space.

Colour starts from three L1 anchors: blue for General AI, orange for Agentic AI and teal for Physical AI. L3 titles and definitions and within-L3 L4 semantic variation produce related shades and bounded boundary mixtures. Colour does not change a card's human-approved taxonomy assignment. Darker shades do not mean more severe risks.

Node size reflects weighted degree, the sum of incident link weights. Larger nodes are more connected under this graph construction, not necessarily more probable, severe or important. Weighted Louvain communities, calculated with NetworkX 3.5, resolution 1.0 and seed 23, remain diagnostic metadata rather than displayed taxonomy categories.

Evidence and limitations

Each card contains its identifier, bilingual name and definition, L1–L3 hierarchy, optional Facet and Act-type attributes, and retained evidence references. Probability and Severity are reserved fields and have no assigned values. References support interpretation of a risk; their presence does not establish its frequency or severity.

Keyword and application mappings are exploratory aids, not validated classification accuracy or applicability guarantees. Assess the actual system's capabilities, users and deployment context. Graph filters, colours and navigation do not modify the canonical card dataset.

The open dataset is available through the GitHub repository. The exploration page does not repeat a separate Open Dataset download section.
