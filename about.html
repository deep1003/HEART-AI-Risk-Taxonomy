About HEART

Why HEART

AI services combine model outputs, delegated decisions and physical actions, exposing risks across domains. HEART provides a shared, evidence-linked vocabulary for selecting relevant risks, identifying assessment gaps and assigning controls without redefining categories for each service.

Data and structure

The Technical Report's construction baseline comprises 82,971 risk-focused source records: 50,375 scholarly publications, 938 policy and technical reports, and 31,658 patents. These are evidence records, not incidents or risk cards. The published taxonomy contains 622 bilingual cards across 47 categories: General AI (492), Agentic AI (67) and Physical AI (63).

The hierarchy is L0 Responsible AI, L1 operating domain, L2 risk area (System, Interaction or Societal Impact), L3 category and L4 risk card. Each card retains a stable ID, definition, primary hierarchy path and cited evidence.

Methods

Taxonomy construction. Prior research, governance frameworks and safety standards guide top-down category design; source evidence supplies bottom-up risk candidates. The report describes BGE-M3 embeddings, HDBSCAN density-based grouping and class-based TF-IDF keyword summaries, followed by constrained merging, splitting and deduplication. EM-style assignment iteratively matches cards to eligible L3 categories and updates definition-anchored centroids. Expert adjudication determines final classifications, not similarity alone.

Website exploration. English card names and definitions use 1,024-dimensional BGE-M3 embeddings. Links join the undirected union of eight nearest neighbours with cosine similarity ≥ 0.60; ForceAtlas2 positions the graph. Blue, orange and teal anchor the three L1 domains, with semantic shades within L3 categories. Size reflects weighted degree, the sum of link weights. Louvain communities are diagnostic only and do not define colours or taxonomy assignments.

Keywords & applications mapping

Keywords select candidate cards through source-grounded semantic similarity and scope checks; domain keywords use approved L1 assignments. Applications use service-capability rules. These filters highlight conditional relevance, not confirmed incidents or completed risk assessments, and do not alter the taxonomy.

Technical process and AI for Everyone (Korea) example

Keywords. Source-grounded concept names and definitions are embedded with the same pinned BGE-M3 model as the English L4 names and definitions. The 1,024-dimensional vectors are L2-normalised; their dot product gives cosine similarity against all 622 cards. A card is selected when its score meets the concept-specific threshold (currently 0.60–0.75) or it is explicitly included by a documented scope review, unless explicitly excluded. General AI, Agentic AI and Physical AI instead match existing L1 assignments exactly. These retrieval thresholds are separate from the graph's 0.60 link threshold and are not validated accuracy estimates.

Applications. Service capabilities are translated into deterministic applicability rules, not an application-to-card embedding score. Eligible domains are checked first; a card then matches an approved ID, category-ID prefix or term in its English name or definition, with explicit exclusions applied last. The build stores the resulting card IDs; selecting a filter highlights that set without changing taxonomy assignments or running a new classification.

AI for Everyone (Korea). The profile uses announced service plans reported on 4 September 2026 to identify chatbot responses, information search and delegated applications, bookings and payments. Rules admit General and Agentic AI cards, combining prefixes for reliability, privacy, security, inclusion and delegated authority with terms such as “accessibility”, “public service”, “payment” and “booking”. Nine specified cards are excluded; Physical AI is outside the eligible domains. The current result is 295 candidate cards (259 General, 36 Agentic). Agentic risks apply only where delegation is enabled. This is HEART's conditional applicability judgement, not a classification supplied by the news source, evidence of programme failures or a completed assessment of any deployed service.

Reproducible definitions, thresholds and scope corrections are in keyword definitions; scores and selected IDs are in keyword mappings. Application rules are in SCENARIOS and match(), with published memberships in the network data.

How to use it

Select a keyword or application to highlight candidate risks and view its definition and sources. Domain filters use approved L1 assignments; other keywords use definition-to-card similarity with documented scope checks, while applications use capability-based relevance rules. Inactive risks remain pale.

Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, and double-click the background to reset the viewport without clearing filters. Zooming reveals more L4 labels. Hover for a summary; select a node to open its full card on the right, or below the graph on mobile. Zoom and pan are preserved. Use AI risk taxonomy to search or browse the hierarchy. AI risk incidents outlines planned incident monitoring and taxonomy-based analysis; collection is not yet active.

For assessment, define the service boundary, actors and capabilities; confirm applicable cards across inherited domains; then record evidence, tests, controls and accountable owners. Reassess when the service changes. Filters support selection, not a completed risk assessment.

Limits

The taxonomy reflects available published evidence and expert judgement; confidential or deployment-specific failures may be missing. Independent classification accuracy, reviewer agreement and superiority over alternative construction methods are not established. Graph proximity and filters indicate exploratory relevance, not causality or exhaustive coverage. Node size and colour do not measure severity, likelihood or priority. Probability and Severity remain unassigned; application profiles require service-specific validation.
